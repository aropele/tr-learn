# Factors and valuation according to AQR and GMO

Curated reading path built with Claude on 2026-10-15 from AQR's research library (https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research) and GMO's white papers (https://www.gmo.com/europe/research-library/); an index, not a source.

Vanguard says to own the market cheaply and Bridgewater says to balance risk across economic environments. AQR and GMO add what neither covers: returns can be explained by a few systematic premia (value, momentum, quality, trend), and expected returns depend on starting valuations. AQR approaches the topic quantitatively, with peer-reviewed evidence. GMO is a valuation-driven contrarian that argues allocation should move when prices move.

Phases: 1 The factor premia → 2 Trend and risk parity → 3 Valuation and expected returns

For the other views see [Portfolio construction according to Vanguard](../portfolio-construction/README.md) and [according to Bridgewater](../portfolio-construction-bridgewater/README.md).

## Phase 1 — The factor premia

Value, momentum and quality have earned premia over a century and across asset classes. The "Fact, Fiction" papers test the common objections one by one.

## Phase 2 — Trend and risk parity

Trend following has diversified portfolios in equity bear markets. Risk parity rests on leverage aversion, which gives a theoretical basis to the Bridgewater All Weather idea.

| # | Reading | Year | Key message |
|---|---------|------|-------------|
| 5 | [A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing](../../sources/aqr/2017-00-00-century-evidence-on-trend-following-investing/transcription.md) · [original](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf) | 2017 | Trend following across 67 markets since 1880, with positive returns in most large equity drawdowns |
| 6 | [Leverage Aversion and Risk Parity](../../sources/aqr/2012-00-00-leverage-aversion-risk-parity/transcription.md) · [original](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Leverage-Aversion-and-Risk-Parity.pdf) | 2012 | Investors who avoid leverage overpay for risky assets, so levered safe assets earn more per unit of risk |

## Phase 3 — Valuation and expected returns
