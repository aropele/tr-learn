# Portfolio construction according to Bridgewater

Curated reading path built with Claude on 2026-10-15 from Bridgewater's research library (https://www.bridgewater.com/research-and-insights) and third-party copies of papers no longer online there; an index, not a source.

Bridgewater's research on portfolio construction grows from one idea: asset classes respond predictably to shifts in growth and inflation, so a portfolio balanced in risk across those environments needs no forecast. Everything else follows: separate beta from alpha, size each asset by its risk instead of its dollars, diversify across countries as well as asset classes, and test the mix against regimes that recent history did not show.

Phases: 1 Origins → 2 Balance and risk parity → 3 Geographic diversification → 4 Today's portfolio challenges

Each phase points to the numbered readings below. For the Vanguard view of the same questions see [Portfolio construction according to Vanguard](../portfolio-construction/README.md).

## Phase 1 — Origins: beta, alpha and the All Weather idea

All Weather started in 1996 as a portfolio for Ray Dalio's family trust: four economic environments (growth and inflation, each rising or falling vs expectations), equal risk to each, leverage on the low-risk assets to match the return of equities.

## Phase 2 — Balance and risk parity

A 60/40 portfolio gets most of its risk from equities. Risk parity balances risk, not capital, across return sources that react differently to the economic environment.

## Phase 3 — Geographic diversification

Single-country investors have repeatedly lost most of their wealth; an equal-weight mix of countries had shorter and shallower drawdowns. Most portfolios remain concentrated in their home market and the US.
