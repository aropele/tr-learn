Four principles, one ETF portfolio for an Italian client

How Vanguard's research in this archive would guide an independent fee-only adviser who builds an ETF portfolio for a euro investor. Every rule below hangs on one of four principles. Remember the principle and its mechanism, and you can rebuild the rule on the spot.

GoalsClear, appropriate goals set the risk you take.
BalanceAvoid risk the market does not pay you for.
CostThe client keeps only net returns.
DisciplineA plan the client can stick to beats a better one they abandon.

0 · All four principles

The method in one picture

What comes first: the asset classes, or the client's objective?
Principle

The portfolio is the output of a chain that starts from the client, never from the product shelf.

Mechanism

Vanguard models whole return distributions, not point forecasts, because "with point forecasts, the best recommendation would be to allocate 100% to the asset with the highest forecasted return". A distribution forces a trade-off against the client's own risk aversion.

Rule

Goal → preferences and beliefs → methodology → return distributions (VCMM) → optimiser (VAAM) → portfolio. No methodology is better in absolute terms; the objective picks it.

Click a methodology

With the client

Write the goal down first, in an investment policy statement, then choose the method that fits it. For most ETF clients that means market-cap weights with deliberate, documented deviations.

1 · Goals

The stock/bond mix is the decision that matters most

Going from 40% to 80% equity: what grows faster, the average return or the worst year?
Principle

Asset allocation is "the most important determinant of return variability and long-term performance" of a diversified portfolio.

Mechanism

Each step towards equities buys a little more average return with a lot more downside. The goal decides how much downside the client can live through without selling.

Rule

Set the mix from the goal and the worst year the client can tolerate, not from the return they would like.

With the client

Show the worst year in euros on their own capital before they sign. That number is the plan's stress test.

2 · Balance

Start from the market, deviate on purpose

What is the "neutral" portfolio, the one that needs no justification?
Principle

Balance means avoiding uncompensated risk. Diversification removes risk that earns nothing.

Mechanism

Market-cap-weighted portfolios are "the most efficient way to achieve market returns via diversified exposures at low cost". Every tilt away from them (home bias, factors, active funds) adds a risk that needs its own reason. Ad hoc tilts "ignore correlations among assets and can lead to inefficient portfolios".

Rule

Global market weights are the baseline. A deviation needs a belief, a size and a written reason.

With the client

In a review, ask of every holding "what risk is this paying us for?" An ETF that only duplicates another one adds cost and no balance.

Sources: 2022 framework, p. 4 · VAAM (2024), p. 14.

3 · Balance

Bonds: go global, hedged to euro

Adding non-euro bonds to a euro bond portfolio: does it lower volatility, raise it, or depends?
Principle

Imperfectly correlated bond markets lower volatility "without necessarily decreasing its total return". The condition: "the key … is to hedge the currency exposure".

Mechanism

Currency has roughly zero long-run expected return but adds a lot of volatility, and bonds are a low-volatility asset. So, unhedged, the currency swamps the bond. Hedged, you keep the diversification across yield curves and drop the currency noise.

The more bonds in the portfolio, the more hedging matters and the more global bonds help. Gains arrive "at a decreasing rate" as the global share rises.

Rule

Bond ETFs for a euro client: global aggregate, EUR-hedged share class. Hedging costs about 0.02–0.04% a year in bid-ask spread (Fig. 11). There is "no 'right-size' allocation" to global bonds; up to market cap (79% outside the euro) the volatility keeps falling.

Italian angle

Italy's 10-year yield changes correlate only 0.53 with Germany's, 0.70 with France's and 0.39 with Australia's (Fig. 4). For a client whose bonds are mostly BTPs, going global is diversification, not a cosmetic change.

Outside the sources · verify for Italy
  • Which UCITS global aggregate ETFs offer a EUR-hedged share class, and at what total cost? The mechanism says hedge; the shelf decides whether you can.
  • How does Italian tax treat government bonds held directly compared with bonds held through an ETF? A tax difference is a cost, and cost is a principle.

Source: Going global with bonds (2023, European edition), Figs 1, 4, 9, 11, 13.

4 · Balance

Currency: hedge the bonds, leave equities unhedged

Why would the same investor hedge one asset class and not the other?
Principle

Currency is "a key portfolio decision – not an implementation detail". Decide it by portfolio risk, not by forecasting exchange rates.

Mechanism

Two forces decide: the correlation between currency and asset, and the currency's volatility relative to the asset. Next to a quiet bond, currency noise dominates, so you hedge. Next to volatile equities it is a small share of the risk, and can even diversify, so you leave it.

Rule

"Hedge fixed income and leave equity unhedged." The more equity, the lower the overall hedge ratio. For the euro, correlation has been risk-adding in 44% of periods and "has recently hovered around zero" (Fig. 4, p. 6), so forecasting it is a weak basis for a decision.

With the client

When the dollar falls and the global equity ETF lags, the explanation is already written: the currency stays in equities on purpose. Avoid "over‑engineering solutions to an inherently uncertain problem".

Source: The FX dimension (May 2026, EU edition), Figs 2–4, 7, appendix, p. 7.

5 · Balance

Equity: global, and home bias is a bet

An Italian client holding mostly European equities: is that the cautious choice or the active one?
Principle

The same as for bonds: the market portfolio is neutral, and anything else is a position.

Mechanism

The eurozone is about 9% of global equity (Fig. 2). A portfolio of 50% European equity is a five-fold overweight on one region: concentrated risk the market does not reward. Diversified composites have narrower outcome ranges than any single region (VCMM Fig. 7).

Rule

Core equity = global market-cap ETF, unhedged. A home tilt, if any, is a sized and documented decision, for example to hold some liabilities in euro.

With the client

Reframe "I know Italian companies" as a bet that is 9% of the world against 91%. Then let them choose its size.

Sources: FX dimension, Fig. 2 · VCMM (2026), Fig. 7 · reading 10 in the theme index (not yet archived).

6 · Cost

Index or active: a four-variable test, not a belief

An active fund expected to beat the index by 0.8% a year but costing 1.0%. In or out?
Principle

Vanguard "reject[s] this basic, binary choice". Active needs "talent, low costs, and patience". Cost is "the enemy of net alpha".

Mechanism

Only net alpha reaches the client: gross alpha minus cost. If it is zero or negative, active only adds tracking error. If it is positive, the mix depends on how much active risk the client tolerates, and an index core then "diversifies" manager risk.

Rule

No skill, or net alpha ≤ 0: all index. For an ETF portfolio that is the default, and it is usually the end of the discussion.

With the client

Answer "why not a manager who beats the market?" with the four variables, not with an opinion on active management.

7 · Cost

Costs compound against the client

0.7% versus 2.0% a year in costs, over 30 years: roughly how much of the final capital disappears?
Principle

"Clients only get to keep, spend or bequest net returns." Cost is the one return driver you control with certainty.

Mechanism

Leakage of fees, taxes and trading is taken every year from a growing base, so the gap widens exponentially, not linearly.

Rule

Choose the cheapest ETF that delivers the exposure you decided on. Then look at the leakage you cannot see on the factsheet: spreads, taxes, unnecessary trades.

With the client

Your fee is leakage too. A fee-only adviser earns it by cutting the other leakages and by preventing the big behavioural ones (sections 9–10).

Outside the sources · verify for Italy

  • Stamp duty and the custody costs of the platform are part of leakage. Include them in the same yearly figure.

8 · Discipline

Expectations are ranges, and the tails are fat

How often has US equity lost money over a full decade: almost never, or a few percent of the time?
Principle

"Uncertainty is the point, not a limitation." "History informs but does not dictate."

Mechanism

Returns are "compensation for bearing systematic risk", built in layers. Bad decades happen more often than a normal curve suggests: the S&P 500 was negative in 3% of rolling 10-year windows since 1871, against under 0.4% under a normal distribution.

Long-run building blocks (VCMM steady state, Fig. 5) Chance of a 10-year return below −1% (Fig. 8, approx.)

Rule

Quote a range, never a single number. Update expectations every year (Vanguard's outlook comes out each December), but change the allocation only if the client's goal changed.

With the client

Name the bad decade before it happens. A client warned in advance is far less likely to sell at the bottom.

Source: Vanguard Capital Markets Model (Sep 2026), p. 3, Figs 5, 8, p. 12. US DATA

9 · Discipline

Rebalance to keep the risk you chose

Rebalancing a 60/40 portfolio: does it raise returns or control risk?
Principle

Rebalancing keeps the portfolio at the risk the goal requires. It is a risk tool, not a return tool.

Mechanism

Left alone, the higher-returning asset grows into a larger share: a 60/40 drifts towards an 80/20 risk level. The drifting portfolio earned more only because it took more risk. At equal risk, the rebalanced portfolio wins by about 12 bps.

Printed values, Fig. III-2.
Rule

Send new money, coupons and dividends to the most underweight asset first. If trading costs are fixed, rebalance to target. If they are proportional (commissions, taxes), rebalance only to the nearest edge of the band.

With the client

Rebalancing means buying what has just fallen. Agree the rule in writing while markets are calm.

Outside the sources · verify for Italy
  • In the Italian tax regime the client uses, does each sale realise a taxable gain? If so, taxes are proportional costs and the rule points to band-edge rebalancing driven by cash flows.

10 · Discipline

Where a fee-only adviser's value comes from

Of the adviser's possible value-add, which single item is the largest: fund selection, rebalancing or coaching?
Principle

Do not sell market-beating returns: that depends on things outside your control. Sell what is "well within an adviser's control".

Mechanism

The value is lumpy. It arrives "intermittently, often during periods of market duress or euphoria", when one prevented mistake is worth "tens of percentage points … rather than mere basis points". In the 2020 crash, a 60/40 investor who stayed invested made 62%; one who went to cash at the bottom made 37% (Fig. 6).

Adviser's Alpha, upper end of each range (bps a year, Fig. 2) Asset allocation and total-return investing are rated "> 0" but too client-specific to quantify. The total is "up to, or exceeding, 3%" and does not arrive evenly each year. Asset location and withdrawal order are measured on UK wrappers. UK
Rule

Plan (investment policy statement and the best-fit allocation), implement cheaply, keep deviations from the market small, and coach the client before the turbulence. Moving to fee-based pay "align[s] adviser and client interests".

11 · Synthesis

Checklist for an Italian client

  1. Goal, written in an investment policy statement, with the worst year the client accepts (§1).
  2. Stock/bond mix from that goal and loss tolerance (§1).
  3. Equity core: one global market-cap ETF, unhedged; any home tilt sized and written down (§2, §5).
  4. Bond core: global aggregate ETF, EUR-hedged, global share up to market weight (§3).
  5. Currency: check the overall hedge ratio falls as equity rises (§4).
  6. Active or factor sleeves only if they pass the four-variable test (§6).
  7. Leakage: ETF costs, spreads, taxes and your fee in one yearly figure (§7).
  8. Rules agreed in calm markets: rebalancing bands, how to use cash flows, what you will do in a crash (§8–§10).
